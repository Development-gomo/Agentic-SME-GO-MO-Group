# AI and Ethics: Shaping the Future of Business

_Updated 9 November 2026_

![](https://www.gomogroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Screenshot-2023-11-09-at-11.11.11.png)

> A Vision for Integrating Generative AI Ethics into the Core of Business and Society

Generative AI has emerged not just as an engine of creativity but as the catalyst for a surge in innovation that has been compared to a new Industrial Revolution. According to McKinsey’s report “The State of AI in 2023”, published in August 2023, organisations were rapidly deploying generative AI tools and expected significant effects on their industries and workforces. The choices we make now will set the course for a long time to come. With that much power comes an equally great responsibility, which is why AI and ethics is something every company using the technology needs to take a position on.

## What do AI and ethics mean for companies?

AI ethics is about using AI in a way that is transparent, fair and secure, with people taking responsibility for the outcome. For a company, that means clear rules on how AI may be used, what data may be entered and who reviews what the AI produces.

Ethical AI, or responsible AI, is usually described through five principles:

- **Transparency:** it should be clear when AI is used and how it affects a decision or a text.
- **Fairness:** outputs are reviewed so that the AI does not reinforce biases in the data it is built on.
- **Privacy and security:** personal data and trade secrets are protected, including when they are used in AI tools.
- **Accountability:** a person or function is responsible for how AI is used and for what it produces.
- **Human oversight:** people review, and can stop or change, what the AI proposes.

In practice, AI ethics only becomes concrete in an AI policy that employees actually use. At GO MO Group, we provide [support with AI strategy and policy](https://www.gomogroup.com/en/solution/generative-ai-offering/) as part of our generative AI work.

## What does the EU AI Act require of companies today?

The EU AI Act is being introduced in stages. For most companies that use AI, three things apply today: some uses are prohibited, staff should be supported in building AI literacy, and people must be told when they encounter AI or AI-generated content.

- **Since 2 February 2025**, certain uses of AI have been prohibited, such as social scoring of people, emotion recognition in workplaces and schools, and manipulation that exploits people’s vulnerabilities. The AI literacy requirement for staff who use AI has applied from the same date.
- **Since 27 July 2026**, the amending Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, known as the Digital Omnibus on AI, has been in force. It softened the AI literacy requirement: companies must now take measures to support their staff’s AI literacy, rather than ensure a certain level.
- **Since 2 August 2026**, the transparency rules in Article 50 have applied. People must be told when they are interacting with an AI system, AI-generated content must be marked, and deepfakes must be disclosed as such.
- **From 2 December 2027**, the rules for stand-alone high-risk systems apply, for example AI used in recruitment or credit assessment. For AI built into products that are already regulated, such as machinery and medical devices, the rules apply from 2 August 2028.

A first step is therefore knowing where AI is used in the business, who uses it and how staff are trained. Documented [training in responsible AI use](https://www.gomogroup.com/en/case/generative-ai-from-hype-to-real-business-value/) can be one of the measures that support AI literacy.

## Generative AI: a new epoch of innovation

Generative AI creates new content instead of only analysing what already exists, which is why the ethical questions grow larger. This is not the AI of bygone days. Generative AI doesn’t just simulate, it innovates. As McKinsey’s 2023 report highlighted, generative AI is becoming a cornerstone in the evolution of various sectors. As it redefines the parameters of creation, we face the task of guiding this force with a moral compass, ensuring it serves the greater good and not just the few.

## Charting an ethical course

Charting an ethical course means steering generative AI towards uses that uplift and unite, and that takes dialogue between the people who use the technology. GO MO Group’s annual summit in Pune is one of the ways we drive that dialogue. Here, leaders come together to make ethical consideration part of AI’s progress. At the 2023 summit, we were honoured to host Indian CEOs and Managing Directors from multinational companies such as Alfa Laval, IKEA and Sandvik, along with other members of the Indo-Swedish industrial community. Their participation showed the summit’s importance and a shared commitment to the ethical deployment of generative AI.

Echoing the findings of McKinsey’s 2023 report, our dialogue centred on the industry-wide effects of generative AI and how it is reshaping the workforce. Together with these leaders, we are shaping a future that acknowledges the depth of generative AI’s impact on global business and the way we work.

## Conclusion

Generative AI requires both innovation and vigilance, and the standards we set today will determine how the technology is judged in the future. The 2023 Pune summit helped us keep our course towards an ethical approach. As McKinsey’s report “The State of AI in 2023” asserts, the industry is poised for a paradigm shift, one that GO MO Group is committed to leading with clear ethical standards.
