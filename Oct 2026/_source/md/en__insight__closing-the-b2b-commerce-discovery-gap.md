# Closing the B2B Commerce Discovery Gap: When Machines Visit Before Humans Do

_The next trillion users of the internet aren't human. Most B2B companies are invisible to them._

![](https://www.gomogroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/closing-the-b2b-commerce-discovery-gap-gomo-merriweather.webp)

## The majority of your website's visitors can't see it

57.8% of all HTTP requests to web content are now automated. For the first time in the thirty-year history of the commercial internet, machines outnumber humans as the primary audience of the web.

Your website was built for people. Optimized for people. Budgeted for people. But the majority of visitors arriving at your digital front door are AI agents, LLMs, and automated procurement systems — and most of them leave empty-handed, because your site speaks a language they weren't designed to understand.

**This is the B2B Commerce Discovery Gap:** the growing divide between companies that are discoverable by both humans and machines, and companies that are invisible to the fastest-growing buyer on the internet.

The paradox is that human-to-human connection in B2B matters more than ever. Trust, relationships, the handshake behind the deal — none of that has lost value. But if the machine filtered you out before a human conversation could start, none of it gets the chance to matter. The imperative is clear: build for both.

![](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns='http://www.w3.org/2000/svg'%20viewBox='0%200%201713%20918'%3E%3C/svg%3E)

## The shift is faster than anything we've seen

How fast did this happen? Faster than social shopping. Faster than e-commerce search. Faster than mobile.

Bain & Company's 2026 research confirms it: 44% of US online buyers now start their product discovery journey inside an LLM — or split their search between AI tools and traditional search engines. This isn't generational fringe behavior. While adoption is roughly twice as fast among Gen Z and millennials, it has spread across every age group. And it has now reached B2B markets.

![](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns='http://www.w3.org/2000/svg'%20viewBox='0%200%201488%20816'%3E%3C/svg%3E)

Our own data at GO MO Group and Collaboration Art, drawn from access to over 600 clients across the Nordics, tells the same story. The shift to AI-mediated search has moved faster than any digital behavior change we've tracked in a decade. Marketers who view this as merely an update to SEO are, to borrow Bain's language, "already behind the curve."

## The shortlist is built before a human gets involved

Here is where it gets consequential for anyone in B2B commerce.

Qualitative research indicates that buyers at small and medium-size businesses have already started to build their vendor shortlists using AI agents and LLMs. They're using AI to construct the consideration set — then turning to websites, review platforms, and YouTube demonstrations to validate what the model suggests.

**Agents, LLMs, and humans — three fundamentally different audiences — now shape the same buying journey.** The machines construct the shortlist. The human validates it.

> If your brand doesn't surface in that first AI-generated shortlist, you're not losing a ranking. You're losing the entire pipeline before a human ever gets involved.

This is just the beginning. We expect significant disruption in the digital commerce and marketing funnel as these behaviors migrate into high-value enterprise deals. CMOs with enterprise business customers should be preparing for this now — next year is probably too late. Reactiveness in proven digital opportunities has shown to be very expensive in B2B. For the majority of our clients, being a first digital mover is a genuine competitive advantage.

## The sources that matter have changed

An analysis of roughly 500 million citations from the AI search platform [ScrunchAI](https://www.linkedin.com/company/scrunchai/) showed that 89% of unbranded prompts are fulfilled by third-party sources — not the brand's own website, blog, or paid ads. The dispersion by topic ranges from 76% to 99%.

![](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns='http://www.w3.org/2000/svg'%20viewBox='0%200%201685%20934'%3E%3C/svg%3E)

What does this mean? The sources that LLMs and AI agents trust look more like a strong PR and earned media strategy than a performance marketing dashboard. Third-party review sites, industry publications, analyst commentary, and affiliate content dominate. Your home page — built for human eyes — barely registers.

And there's a practical reason for that. A traditional web page requires rendering HTML, CSS, JavaScript, images, and interactive elements — significant computing power for an AI system to parse and interpret. A parallel agentic layer — structured data served via APIs, JSON, [Schema.org](http://schema.org/), llms.txt — delivers the same information at a fraction of the computational cost. When an AI agent evaluates ten thousand suppliers, it won't render ten thousand websites. It'll query the ones that made their data machine-readable. The parallel layer isn't a nice-to-have. It's how your home page starts to register in the new discovery layer.

This is a structural reset in marketing resource allocation. The traditional lower-funnel playbook — great SEO, SEM, conversion optimization — remains fundamental and key, yet no longer sufficient on its own. The brands winning in AI-driven discovery are investing in three things: category fame — evergreen, thought-leading content that isn't manufactured for search bots alone; transparent brand portrayal — real pricing, real product information, minimal fluff — combined with content freshness that AI crawlers can parse cleanly; and a parallel layer of the website — a programmatic, agentic interface built for machine consumption alongside the human experience.

## Procurement systems are going agentic too

The bot traffic story gets more concrete, and more urgent, when you look at enterprise procurement.

We've spent the past several months researching the five largest procurement platforms in the world: SAP Ariba, Coupa, GEP SMART, Oracle Fusion, and Ivalua. Every single one of them is embedding agentic AI into their supplier evaluation workflows.

SAP Ariba's Joule AI evaluates suppliers across the world's largest procurement network — over 5 million connected companies. Coupa's AI draws on $9.5 trillion in community spend data to benchmark and recommend. GEP's agentic AI (GEP Qi) handles autonomous sourcing. Oracle Fusion's Autonomous Sourcing Assistant identifies, invites, negotiates, and awards bids — automatically.

**These systems don't visit your website. They don't read your brand story. They query structured data.**

This is the part most B2B leaders are missing entirely. While marketing teams debate prompt optimization, procurement departments are deploying AI agents that evaluate, rank, and shortlist suppliers without a single human clicking a link. If your company's product data isn't structured, machine-readable, and accessible via APIs — you're invisible to the fastest-growing buyer in the market: the machine.

## The infrastructure shift beneath the surface

Databricks' 2026 "State of AI Agents" report — built from telemetry across more than 20,000 organizations, including over 60% of the Fortune 500 — puts hard numbers on what's happening beneath the surface.

Multi-agent workflows grew 327% in just four months. AI agents now create 80% of all new databases. 97% of database testing environments are built by agents. These are not experiments. These are production systems that reason, plan, and execute autonomously.

![](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns='http://www.w3.org/2000/svg'%20viewBox='0%200%201798%20875'%3E%3C/svg%3E)

And here's the kicker: 40% of enterprise AI use cases already focus on customer experience and engagement — customer support, advocacy, onboarding, personalized content. The machines aren't just crawling your content. They're interacting with your customers, evaluating your competitors, and building the consideration sets that determine who gets the deal.

The content-for-traffic model is structurally breaking down

There's a metric we keep coming back to: the crawl-to-refer ratio. It measures how many pages an AI system fetches from your site for every human visitor it sends back.

- **Google Search: 5 to 1.** For every five pages crawled, one human click.
- **OpenAI: 857 to 1.** Eight hundred and fifty-seven pages consumed for every single referral.
- **Anthropic's Claude: 23,951 to 1.**

![](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns='http://www.w3.org/2000/svg'%20viewBox='0%200%201749%20899'%3E%3C/svg%3E)

All AI chatbots combined — ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity — account for just 0.29% of all search referral traffic.

> The machines are consuming your content at unprecedented scale and returning almost nothing in traditional traffic. The content-for-traffic model that funded two decades of digital marketing is structurally breaking down.

This is precisely why the ROI equation has fundamentally shifted — and why the winners are investing in evergreen thought-leading content, transparent brand portrayal, and a parallel agentic layer. Content still matters enormously. But its purpose has expanded: it feeds the models that build the shortlists, and your structured data is what agents query when the deal is on the line. If neither is in place, you simply don't exist in the new discovery layer.

## What B2B growth leaders should be asking right now

The companies that will own the next decade of B2B customer acquisition are not the ones with the best Google rankings. They're the ones building the infrastructure to be discovered, evaluated, and selected by both humans and machines — simultaneously.

Three questions every growth leader should be able to answer today:

**Where are your next customers actually starting their search?** Traditional digital marketing — strong SEO, paid search, conversion-optimized websites — remains the foundation. That hasn't changed, and it shouldn't. But if you believe that foundation alone is enough, you're missing where a growing share of your prospects now begin: inside AI-driven discovery, where the shortlist is built before a single link is clicked.

**Can a machine evaluate your company as a supplier?** Not read about it — evaluate it. Structured data, APIs, machine-readable product specifications, real-time pricing. If the only way to understand your offering is to navigate a website designed for humans, you've excluded the majority audience.

**Are you shaping your own shortlist, or are your competitors?** In AI-driven discovery, 89% of what the model cites comes from sources you don't control. Your earned media strategy, your presence in third-party reviews, and your accuracy across the web now determine whether you make the cut.

## What happens next depends on what you do now

The data is definitive. The commercial internet has shifted from a human-first medium to a dual-audience infrastructure, and the gap between companies that are visible to machines and those that aren't is widening every quarter.

The next trillion users of the internet are not human. We spent thirty years building the web for people — designed to persuade, inform, convert. That era isn't ending. But it's no longer the whole story. The web now has two audiences with fundamentally different needs, and most companies are only built for one of them.

The companies closing the B2B Commerce Discovery Gap today share three things: they've audited their machine-readability, they're building a parallel agentic layer alongside their existing web presence, and they're investing in the earned media and structured data that AI systems actually trust.

**The machine makes the shortlist. The human makes the call. Build for both.**
