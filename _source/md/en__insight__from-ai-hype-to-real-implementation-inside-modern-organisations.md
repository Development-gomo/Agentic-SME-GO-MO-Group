# From AI hype to real implementation inside modern organisations

In this conversation, [**Henrik Andereberg**](https://www.gomogroup.com/en/team/henrik-anderberg/), digital strategist at [**GO MO Group**](https://www.gomogroup.com/en/), explores how generative AI has evolved from early hype to a powerful technological shift already reshaping knowledge work. Reflecting on the rapid rise of tools like ChatGPT, he discusses why AI adoption inside companies still lags behind technological progress, the growing divide between early adopters and organisations, and what leaders must do to move from experimentation to real implementation across teams and workflows.

Since the emergence of tools like ChatGPT in 2022, generative AI has advanced rapidly. What began as experimentation is now entering daily workflows, though organizational adoption is still early.

Generative AI is more than a productivity tool. It is reshaping knowledge work, with intelligent systems supporting analysis, generating insights, and assisting decisions while workers increasingly guide and oversee these systems.

> “Technologically the progress has been incredibly fast. But adoption within companies is still surprisingly slow.”

## The great divide in AI adoption

Despite rapid technological progress, **AI adoption across organizations remains uneven**. Some individuals already use AI tools daily, integrating them into their workflows and decision making. **Many companies, however, are still in early experimentation stages**. This creates a **growing divide between those actively leveraging AI and those still observing from the sidelines**, where the technology exists and works, but its benefits are not yet widely realised.

![](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns='http://www.w3.org/2000/svg'%20viewBox='0%200%20960%20540'%3E%3C/svg%3E)

## Three stages of AI adoption

Organisations are currently adopting generative AI at very different speeds. While some companies remain in early experimentation, others are beginning to scale usage across teams and workflows. Broadly, AI adoption tends to evolve through three stages, moving from isolated experimentation to structured rollout and eventually to deep integration into everyday operations.

1\. What does early stage AI experimentation begin in companies?

AI adoption often starts organically. Individuals experiment with tools independently without a company wide strategy. While this helps teams discover possibilities, the overall impact remains limited without leadership direction.

2\. How do companies go from experimentation to structured AI adoption?

Organisations begin introducing training, leadership support, and clear expectations for daily AI use. This helps move from isolated experimentation to broader adoption across teams and workflows.

3\. What happens when AI becomes fully integrated in business?

AI becomes embedded across workflows, systems, and decision making. Organizations redesign how work is performed, turning AI from a tool into a core part of operations.

## Why companies struggle with adoption

One of the biggest **barriers to AI adoption is not the technology itself but organisational leadership**. Many companies experiment with small pilot initiatives without establishing a clear direction for broader transformation. Without strategic leadership and structured implementation, the impact of AI often remains limited.

> “One of the biggest challenges is what I call ‘incremental leadership’. Instead of making AI a central priority, companies give a few people licenses here and there. That does not change the organization.”

## The GO MO approach to leadership in the age of AI

Generative AI technology has advanced rapidly, but the **real challenge now lies in organisational adoption**. Many companies are still experimenting with isolated tools, yet meaningful transformation requires leadership that sets clear priorities and guides teams from experimentation to everyday use.

From a [**GO MO Group**](https://www.gomogroup.com/en/) perspective, successful organisations approach AI **adoption as a structured capability building journey**. When leadership commitment is combined with training, experimentation, and practical use cases, AI moves beyond hype and becomes a driver of productivity, innovation, and long term competitive advantage.
