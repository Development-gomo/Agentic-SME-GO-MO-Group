# How to win organically on LinkedIn, according to LinkedIn

_On LinkedIn, B2B engagement is earned by people and proof. The company page comes second._

![](https://www.gomogroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/linkedin-organic-featured-image.webp)

LinkedIn has changed how its algorithm decides what shows up in the feed. We went through what LinkedIn itself has published in 2026, from engineering posts to marketing guides and buyer research, and turned it into a short guide.

The short version: post from people, stick to a few topics, and show real proof. Here's why, and how to do it.

## How the LinkedIn feed works, in plain words

LinkedIn's engineering team explained the new feed system in March 2026 ( [LinkedIn Engineering](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/feed/engineering-the-next-generation-of-linkedins-feed)). It works in two steps.

STEP 1 · FIND →Matches each post to members who might carePost + author's name, headline, company and industry, compared with the member's profile and history

STEP 2 · RANK →1,000+past interactions per member: long reads, likes, comments and shares

ON TOP · FILTER−40%views of "AI slop" after LinkedIn's recent efforts

**Step 1:** LinkedIn finds posts that might interest each member. It reads every post together with the author's name, headline, company and industry, and compares it with the member's profile and the posts they have engaged with before. That is how a post can reach people well outside the author's own network.

**Step 2:** LinkedIn ranks those posts. It looks at more than a thousand of each member's past interactions, such as posts they read for a long time, liked, commented on or shared, and predicts what they will find useful next.

On top of that, LinkedIn filters generic content. In September 2026, LinkedIn said its recent efforts had cut views of what it calls "AI slop", meaning posts that "may sound polished on the surface but lacks a clear point of view, unique perspective, or substance", by 40% ( [LinkedIn](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/how-linkedin-is-tackling-ai-slop)).

**What this means for you:** the feed favors posts from people with a clear topic, read properly by the right audience.

## Three things that work on LinkedIn right now

### 1\. Post from people, with the company page in support

LinkedIn's own data shows that the same content gets roughly twice the engagement when a person shares it, compared with a company page. Yet only 3% of employees regularly share their company's content ( [LinkedIn B2B Institute](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/b2b-research/trends/the-drip-effect)).

2xthe engagement when a person shares the same content, compared with a company page.

**In practice:** let two or three experts do most of the posting from their own profiles. The company page shares their posts and documents cases, methods and events. LinkedIn is also rolling out collaborative posts, which let a company page invite up to five people onto one post ( [LinkedIn](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/introducing-collaborative-posts-now-available-to-members-and-company-pages-on-linkedin)).

### 2\. Stick to a few topics

Since LinkedIn matches posts to readers by topic and by the author's headline, an expert who posts about the same few subjects week after week gives the feed a clear signal about who should see the work. This is our reading of how the system works: it rewards consistent, credible coverage of a topic, much as search engines do.

**In practice:** give each expert one theme, and make their headline match it.

### 3\. Show real proof

LinkedIn's Buyability guide reports that 95% of B2B contracts go to a vendor that was on the buyer's list from day one. When buyers were asked why they felt confident in a purchase, the most common answer was that they "could defend the decision if it went wrong" ( [LinkedIn, Buyability guide](https://delivery-p143253-e1476319.adobeaemcloud.com/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:f733b42a-1409-4285-971a-3337b707d354/original/as/Building_More_Buyable_B2B_Brands.pdf)).

This research is about how B2B buyers choose vendors in general, so the link to LinkedIn posts is our interpretation. In our view, it explains why proof works so well in the feed: customer results, named experts and real decisions make a company easy to remember and easy to defend.

**In practice:** build every post around one real insight, decision or result from your own work.

## Your five-step plan for next week

1. **Choose three core themes.** Pick the topics you want buyers to connect with your company, in your customers' own words.
2. **Give each theme one expert.** Each expert posts from their own profile, with a headline that matches the theme.
3. **Publish two or three posts a week.** That's the pace [LinkedIn's 2026 guide to AI search visibility](https://delivery-p143253-e1476319.adobeaemcloud.com/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:6c0e651c-f96f-4581-ae6d-f4a14238fba8/original/as/Unlocking%20AI%20Search%20Visibility%20-%20The%20B2B%20Marketer%27s%20Guide%20to%20LinkedIn%202026.pdf) recommends. Pick a rhythm your team can keep, with time to answer comments.
4. **Build every post on proof.** Ask the expert for a decision they made, a number that shows what happened and a lesson they would pass on.
5. **Measure the right signals, and review after 30 days.** Track comments from your target audience, profile visits and views from outside your network, alongside likes.

## What a good post looks like

LinkedIn's August 2026 guide for B2B marketers gives advice that helps both readers in the feed and AI search. It recommends building the first line around your topic and keeping that line free of hashtags, emojis and special characters, putting the takeaway first, keeping to one idea, aiming for 200-300 words and replacing marketing jargon with the terms your customers use ( [LinkedIn](https://delivery-p143253-e1476319.adobeaemcloud.com/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:6c0e651c-f96f-4581-ae6d-f4a14238fba8/original/as/Unlocking%20AI%20Search%20Visibility%20-%20The%20B2B%20Marketer%27s%20Guide%20to%20LinkedIn%202026.pdf)).

## Popular rules worth testing yourself

Several LinkedIn "rules" come from outside LinkedIn's own material, so treat them as ideas to test on your own page:

- **The "60% link penalty".** LinkedIn's material leaves the effect of links open. If you link, put the takeaway in the post itself.
- **The "first hour".** LinkedIn's engineers describe ranking based on each member's interests and history, balancing freshness with relevance.
- **Exact weights for likes, comments and saves.** LinkedIn keeps these to itself.

"Don't fall for universal 'best practices'. What works for one brand might not work for yours. It's crucial to test and gather your own data."

As Cassie Dell, LinkedIn's Head of Organic Growth, puts it in LinkedIn's B2B guide

## LinkedIn algorithm: frequently asked questions

Why is my LinkedIn reach dropping?

LinkedIn rebuilt its feed in 2026 and now filters out generic content more strictly. In September it said its efforts had cut views of "AI slop" by 40%. LinkedIn's own material points to three things that decide who sees a post: the algorithm reads each post together with the author's headline, company and industry and shows it to people interested in that topic; it ranks posts on more than a thousand of each member's past interactions, such as long reads, comments and shares; and the same content gets roughly twice the engagement when a person shares it, compared with a company page. In our view, posts with a clear topic and first-hand proof from a named expert match what the feed now rewards.

How does the LinkedIn algorithm decide who sees my posts?

In two steps, according to LinkedIn's engineering team (March 2026). First it finds posts that might interest each member, reading the post together with the author's name, headline, company and industry. Then it ranks them using more than a thousand of each member's past interactions. That's why a clear topic and a matching headline help the right people see a post.

Do we still need a company page?

Yes. LinkedIn's guide recommends "a well-managed Page with employees who publish quality content regularly". In our view, the page is where buyers check who you are, and the experts bring the reach.
