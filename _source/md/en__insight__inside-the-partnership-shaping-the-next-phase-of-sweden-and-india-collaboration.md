# Inside the partnership shaping the next phase of Sweden and India collaboration

Sweden and India share a long standing relationship shaped by diplomacy, trade, and innovation. In this conversation, Swedish Ambassador to India **Jan Thesleff** reflects on how the partnership is evolving through sustainability, co creation, and knowledge exchange, and what this means for the next phase of collaboration between the two countries.

### Foundation of the Sweden India relationship

Sweden and India have more than 75 years of diplomatic relations, supported by even deeper trade and people to people connections. Several Swedish companies have operated in India for over 120 years, reflecting a longstanding economic partnership built on trust, continuity, and shared industrial growth.

> “One of the mottos for our presence here in India is Swedish innovation made in India. We see more and more Swedish companies establishing centers of excellence, cooperations with universities, and research departments with Indian talent.”

### Sustainability at the core of collaboration

Sustainability is shaping the evolving economic relationship between Sweden and India. As companies from both countries expand across markets, growth is increasingly aligned with environmental responsibility, climate innovation, and long term industrial transformation. With strong business presence in both regions, sustainability now sits at the centre of collaboration, investment, and future industrial priorities.

**Key partnership indicators**

- 280 Swedish companies actively operating across industries in India
- 70 Indian companies expanding presence and partnerships in Sweden

### Future collaboration vision

Looking ahead, the partnership between Sweden and India is expected to move beyond traditional trade toward deeper co creation and knowledge driven collaboration. The focus is increasingly on building together through research, innovation, academic exchange, startups, and sustainability cooperation, shaping a platform for the next 75 years of shared growth.

> “There will be much more focus on co creation, the knowledge economy, and on building things together rather than simply sending goods between countries.”

### How GO MO sees this shift

As international partnerships deepen and more companies expand across markets, visibility and positioning become critical. When organisations invest in innovation, research, and long term collaboration, their ability to communicate that value clearly across digital channels directly influences growth, perception, and competitive advantage.
