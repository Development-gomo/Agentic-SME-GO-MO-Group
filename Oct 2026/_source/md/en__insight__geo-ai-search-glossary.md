## **Section 1: The 12 Core Acronyms & Disciplines Every Marketer Must Know**

These are the foundational terms — the ones that appear in every strategy deck, audit, and client briefing in 2026.

Core

### **GEO — Generative Engine Optimization**

The practice of structuring content and managing online presence to improve visibility in responses generated by AI systems such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, and Claude. GEO focuses on making content the preferred source that generative AI draws upon when answering queries. Originated in a 2023 Princeton/Georgia Tech/IIT Delhi research paper demonstrating 30–40% visibility uplift from optimized content.

**PRO TIP:** GEO is not just about being found — it's about being cited. The goal is AI attribution, not just a ranking.

Core

### AEO — Answer Engine Optimization

Structuring on-page content so an answer engine can extract a clean, standalone passage and serve it as a direct answer. Core tactics include question-led H2s, one-sentence direct replies beneath each heading, short definitional paragraphs, and schema markup. AEO emerged as Google began displaying featured snippets and knowledge panels. GEO and AEO are largely synonymous — AEO focuses on extractable passages, GEO covers the broader generative surface.

Core

### LLMO — Large Language Model Optimization

Also called LLM Optimization. The practice of influencing how large language models understand, recall, and represent your brand — both in training data and in live retrieval. Overlaps heavily with GEO but emphasises entity-level brand presence across the full LLM ecosystem, not just individual citations.

**PRO TIP:** LLMO requires consistent entity signals across Wikipedia, press coverage, and structured data — not just on-page copy.

Core

### AIO — AI Optimization / AI Overviews Optimization

A term with two common meanings. Some marketers use AIO as a catch-all for every AI search tactic. Others use it specifically for optimizing content to appear inside Google's AI Overviews feature. Always verify context before building a strategy around this acronym — it's being used inconsistently across the industry in 2026.

Core

### AI SEO — AI Search Engine Optimization

The umbrella term for any SEO practice aimed at improving visibility across AI-powered search surfaces — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Mode, and beyond. AI SEO encompasses AEO, GEO, LLMO, and AIO as component disciplines rather than replacing them. Think of it as the new category label for "modern search marketing."

Core

### SEO — Search Engine Optimization (Evolved)

The original practice of optimizing content so traditional search engines rank it higher in results. In 2026, some industry voices have reframed SEO to mean "Search Everywhere Optimization" — reflecting visibility across Google, social search platforms, and AI-powered results. The foundational principles of quality, authority, and relevance remain unchanged.

Core

### SXO — Search Experience Optimization

The combination of SEO and UX (User Experience). A technically perfect answer served on a slow, cluttered page still loses — because both users and search engines reward the full experience, not just the textual content. SXO treats on-page engagement metrics as ranking signals in their own right.

Core

### E-E-A-T — Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness

Google's quality framework, extended from E-A-T in 2022 to add "Experience" as the first pillar. All four signals are used by both traditional search algorithms and AI systems to evaluate source credibility. First-hand experience signals have become especially valuable in AI search, as LLMs increasingly distinguish between direct knowledge and second-hand reporting.

**PRO TIP:** The first "E" (Experience) is where most content still under-delivers. Add first-hand data, testing notes, and personal observations.

Core

### RAG — Retrieval-Augmented Generation

A technical architecture where an AI system first retrieves relevant documents or passages from a search index (retrieval), then uses that retrieved content to inform its generated answer (augmentation). RAG systems cite real sources and are less prone to hallucination than pure generative models. Most AI search products (Perplexity, ChatGPT Search, Google AI Mode) use RAG-based architectures.

Core

### SGE — Search Generative Experience

Google's original brand name for its AI-generated answer feature, piloted through 2023–2024. Now replaced in the product by the "AI Overviews" and "AI Mode" branding. SGE is still referenced in older articles and research, so understanding it is important for context — especially when reviewing case studies or studies from the 2023–2024 period.

Core

### NLP — Natural Language Processing

The branch of AI that enables machines to understand, interpret, and generate human language. NLP is the foundational technology behind all modern search engines and large language models. Understanding NLP principles helps explain why semantic content, entity optimization, and natural writing styles outperform keyword-stuffed content in AI-era search.

Core

### LLM — Large Language Model

A text-based AI model trained on vast quantities of text data that predicts the next most probable token (word fragment) based on patterns in that training and the context it currently sees. ChatGPT (GPT-4o), Claude, Gemini, and Llama are all LLMs. Understanding LLMs is foundational to every GEO and AEO strategy — they are the engines you are optimizing for.

## Section 2: The 10 AI Platforms & Models You Need to Optimize For

These are the platforms where your content either gets cited — or gets invisible. Know each one's citation behavior and ranking signals.

### The AI Search Ecosystem in 2026

The major platforms where content visibility is won or lost

![](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns='http://www.w3.org/2000/svg'%20viewBox='0%200%201920%20788'%3E%3C/svg%3E)

Platform

### ChatGPT Search

OpenAI's search-integrated mode that fetches live web content via Microsoft Bing (and increasingly Google's index) to supplement training data with current information. Processes approximately 2.5 billion prompts daily, of which 65% qualify as search. Has a 96% lower click-through rate than Google — meaning citations here rarely generate direct traffic, but build brand authority and influence purchasing decisions invisibly.

Platform

### Google AI Overviews

Google's AI-generated answer box powered by Gemini, appearing above traditional search results for a rapidly growing share of queries — expanding from 6.49% of queries in January 2025 to over 25% by mid-2025. The single most impactful platform for brands with existing Google SEO authority, as AI Overviews draw heavily from pages already ranking in the top 10. Reduces organic CTR by an average of 58% for queries where it appears.

**PRO TIP:** Pages already ranking #1–#5 are most likely to be cited in AI Overviews. Strong traditional SEO still feeds GEO here.

Platform

### Google AI Mode

A dedicated AI-first search experience launched at Google I/O 2025, handling complex multi-part queries through "fan-out search" — breaking a single prompt into hundreds of parallel sub-queries and synthesizing the results. AI Mode is distinct from AI Overviews: it replaces the traditional SERP entirely for complex queries, making citation here especially valuable.

Platform

### Perplexity

An AI-native answer engine built around citation-first responses, showing users attributed source links for every claim. Serves 780+ million monthly queries. Perplexity's citation behavior is unusually transparent — making it a primary benchmark platform for AEO/GEO measurement. Notable for its "Perplexity Pages" feature, which generates AI-authored topic pages that frequently cite high-authority sources.

Platform

### Claude (Anthropic)

Anthropic's conversational AI product line. Particularly valued in enterprise and developer contexts for its long-context reasoning capabilities and commitment to accuracy. Claude with Web Search extends its knowledge beyond training data through live retrieval and citation. Claude reportedly places particular emphasis on epistemic precision — content that qualifies claims accurately is more likely to be cited.

Platform

### Gemini / Gemini Advanced

Google's family of large language models powering Google AI Mode, AI Overviews, and the standalone Gemini app. Gemini Advanced is the premium tier with enhanced multi-modal reasoning. Because Gemini drives both AI Overviews and Google AI Mode, optimizing for Gemini has the broadest reach of any single AI platform optimisation effort.

Platform

### Microsoft Copilot

Microsoft's AI assistant built on OpenAI's GPT models and powered by Bing's search index. Integrated into Windows, Edge, and Microsoft 365. Copilot is important for B2B marketers in particular — it operates within the Microsoft ecosystem where many corporate users conduct their research, and its search grounding relies on Bing's index rather than Google's.

Platform

### Generative AI (Definition)

AI systems that produce new content — text, images, audio, code, video — based on patterns learned from training data. The parent category that contains LLMs. Generative AI is the broad technological shift driving the entire GEO discipline. Understanding that these systems generate novel responses (not retrieve pre-written ones) is key to understanding why content strategy must change.

Platform

### AI Agents

AI systems that take multi-step autonomous actions — not just answering questions, but browsing, filling forms, calling APIs, and chaining tasks. OpenAI Operator, Anthropic's Claude computer use, and Google's Project Mariner are early-stage examples. AI agents represent the next frontier of GEO: as agents make purchasing decisions, recommend services, and conduct research autonomously, brand presence in AI memory becomes a direct commercial asset.

**PRO TIP:** 2026 is the year to double down on AI agent visibility — brands cited by agents in research mode will have first-mover advantage.

Platform

### Voice Search / Smart Speakers

Search queries delivered verbally via Siri, Google Assistant, Alexa, or smart home devices. Voice search was the original "answer engine" use case that drove early AEO strategy. In 2026, voice and AI text search are converging — the same structured, direct-answer content formats that win AI citations also win voice search responses. Mobile-first, conversational content serves both.

## Section 3: 11 Technical GEO Infrastructure Terms

The back-end mechanics that determine whether AI systems can access, understand, and cite your content.

Technical

### Schema Markup / Structured Data

Machine-readable labels embedded in your HTML that tell search engines and AI systems exactly what each piece of content represents — an FAQ entry, a product, a review, an author biography, a how-to step. Schema markup (implemented via JSON-LD) significantly improves AI systems' ability to extract accurate, citable information from your pages. FAQ schema is one of the highest-impact single technical changes for AEO.

**PRO TIP:** Implement FAQ, Article, HowTo, and Organization schema as a minimum baseline for any GEO-ready site.

Technical

### LLMs.txt

A proposed Markdown file served at /llms.txt that provides AI crawlers with a curated sitemap of your most important pages, complete with brief descriptions. Analogous to robots.txt for traditional bots but optimized for AI understanding. Helps direct limited AI crawl budget toward your most authoritative content. Adoption is growing but impact remains debated — treat it as low-cost hygiene with upside potential.

Technical

### SSR — Server-Side Rendering

Returning fully rendered HTML from the server rather than relying on JavaScript to populate content in the browser. Most AI crawlers cannot execute JavaScript — meaning that content rendered client-side (via React, Vue, Angular SPA frameworks) may be completely invisible to AI systems. SSR is effectively required for reliable AI indexation and is the single most impactful technical fix for JavaScript-heavy websites targeting GEO visibility.

Technical

### Chunking

The practice of breaking a page into self-contained sections — each answering one discrete question or covering one specific sub-topic — with clear headings. Strong chunking helps retrieval systems identify and extract the precise passage relevant to a query, rather than pulling in surrounding context that adds noise. Well-chunked content has significantly higher citation probability in RAG-based AI systems.

Technical

### Passage Indexing

An AI system's ability to identify and cite specific passages within a long document, rather than attributing the document as a whole. Google introduced passage indexing for traditional search in 2021; it's now standard in AI retrieval. Clear H2 and H3 heading structure, with one topic per section, is the primary enabler of accurate passage-level citation.

Technical

### Entity Recognition

The AI's ability to identify and understand named entities — people, organizations, products, locations, concepts — within your content. Strong entity optimisation through consistent naming conventions, schema markup, and cross-platform presence (Wikipedia, Wikidata, Google Knowledge Graph) helps AI systems accurately understand who and what your content is about, increasing the likelihood of accurate attribution.

Technical

### Knowledge Graph

A structured database of entities and relationships that helps AI systems understand connections between people, brands, topics, and concepts. Google's Knowledge Graph powers Knowledge Panels and informs AI Overviews. Having your brand, key personnel, and products represented in knowledge graphs is a foundational LLMO strategy — it shapes how AI systems "understand" your brand, independent of any individual piece of content.

Technical

### Token Efficiency

In AI content processing, the ability to convey maximum information in minimum tokens (word fragments). AI systems have finite context windows — they cannot process unlimited text. Well-structured, concise content that delivers its key claims in the fewest possible tokens is more likely to be fully processed and cited accurately. Avoid filler phrasing, excessive preambles, and bloated introductions.

Technical

### Inference-Time Retrieval

The process by which a RAG-based AI system fetches live web content at the moment of answering a query (inference time), rather than relying solely on training data. This is how ChatGPT Search, Perplexity, and Google AI Mode provide current information. Content optimized for inference-time retrieval needs to be fast-loading, structured, and consistently published/updated to be crawlable when queries arise.

Technical

### Crawl Budget (AI)

The extent to which an AI crawler will index your site during any given period. Unlike Google, which maintains comprehensive crawl budgets for established sites, AI crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot) are often more selective. LLMs.txt, clear internal linking, fast page speeds, and clean XML sitemaps all help ensure AI crawlers prioritize your most authoritative content over thin or duplicate pages.

Technical

### Programmatic SEO

The practice of generating large numbers of templated pages from a database to target long-tail queries at scale. Still viable in 2026 when the output is genuinely useful and unique — but increasingly risky when pages are thin or near-duplicate, as AI systems apply quality filters that devalue low-value programmatic content. High-quality programmatic pages (with original data, genuine depth) can be powerful citation targets for specific query clusters.

## Section 4: 9 Content Strategy & Format Terms for AI Visibility

How you structure, format, and present content determines whether AI systems can find, extract, and cite it.

Content

### Citation Anchor

A highly specific, quotable fact or insight within a piece of content that AI engines consistently extract and cite across multiple platforms. Original statistics, named methodologies, unique frameworks, and proprietary data points serve as citation anchors. Once established, citation anchors generate ongoing AI visibility independently of new content production — they become durable authority assets.

**PRO TIP:** Commission original research. A single proprietary statistic can become a citation anchor across hundreds of AI responses.

Content

### Answer Density

The ratio of direct, quotable answers to total word count in a piece of content. High-answer-density pages deliver their key claims early and concisely, without extensive padding or keyword-stuffing. AI systems reward answer density because they are optimizing for the user's question, not your SEO metrics. Aim for the shortest path from question to answer — then add depth for human readers.

Content

### Q&A Structuring

Formatting pages as explicit question-answer pairs, with the question as the H2 or H3 heading and the direct answer as the first sentence (or short paragraph) directly beneath it. Q&A structuring is the single most impactful content format change for AEO and AI citation probability. Questions should precisely match how users phrase queries — use "People Also Ask" data and conversational query research to source them.

Content

### Topical Authority

The breadth and depth of coverage your website has on a given subject, as perceived by search engines and AI systems. Sites that cover a topic comprehensively — from introductory definitions through to advanced, specific questions — signal genuine expertise. Topical authority is built through topic clustering: a pillar page (broad overview) supported by multiple cluster pages (specific sub-topics), all internally linked.

Content

### Semantic Coherence

The degree to which all elements of a page consistently address the same core topic without unrelated tangents. GEO rewards focused, semantically coherent content over sprawling keyword-targeting pages that try to rank for unrelated queries through volume. Each page should have one clear topical purpose, answerable in a single sentence.

Content

### Answer Graph

An internally linked content ecosystem that covers a topic comprehensively from multiple angles: definition pages, comparison pages, how-to guides, FAQ clusters, and case studies — all linked together. An answer graph ensures that for any AI query related to your topic area, there is a highly relevant, deeply authoritative page on your site that serves as the ideal citation source.

Content

### Direct Answer / AI Snippet

A short extract that an AI engine quotes directly inside its generated response. Writing self-contained sentences that answer a specific question in isolation (e.g., "GEO stands for Generative Engine Optimization, the practice of...") makes content snippet-ready. Direct answers should be formulated to function as standalone sentences — they will often be cited without surrounding context.

Content

### Epistemic Precision

The degree to which content makes accurately qualified claims with appropriate certainty levels. Saying "studies suggest X in approximately Y% of cases" is more epistemically precise than "X always happens." AI systems — particularly Claude — reward epistemic precision, as it signals rigorous, trustworthy sourcing. Overconfident, unsupported claims reduce citation probability and E-E-A-T signals simultaneously.

Content

### Listicle / Enumerated Content

List-based content with numbered or bulleted items. AI engines extract listicles cleanly because each item has an unambiguous start and end point, making them ideal passage indexing targets. "Top 5 reasons," "7 ways to," and "The 3 main types of" formats consistently outperform prose-only content for AI citation rates, particularly for informational and comparison queries.

**PRO TIP:** Use numbered lists wherever possible — ordinal structure ("3 steps to") signals completeness to AI synthesis engines.

## Section 5: 8 Measurement & Performance Terms for GEO ROI

If you can't measure it, you can't improve it. These are the metrics that actually matter for AI search performance.

Measurement

### GEO Visibility Score

A composite metric representing how often and how prominently your brand appears across AI engine answers for a defined set of target queries. Typically calculated by sampling a representative query set across multiple AI platforms (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude), measuring citation rate, position prominence, and sentiment of each mention. The GEO equivalent of organic search ranking position.

Measurement

### Share of Voice (AI)

Your brand's proportional presence in AI-generated answers for a defined topic area, expressed as a percentage of total mentions across a set of queries. "If someone asks an AI anything about \[your industry\], what % of the time is your brand mentioned?" Share of voice is the strategic metric that GEO optimisation ultimately moves — and the one most closely correlated with AI-influenced purchasing decisions.

**PRO TIP:** Track share of voice across all major AI platforms separately — citation patterns vary significantly by platform.

Measurement

### Citation Rate

The percentage of times your domain is cited as a source across a set of AI-generated responses for target queries. Citation rate is the most direct GEO performance metric. Unlike traditional rankings (binary in/out), citation rate is probabilistic — the same query may return your domain 6 out of 10 times due to AI non-determinism. Measurement requires multiple runs per query.

Measurement

### Competitive Citation Gap

The difference between your citation rate and a competitor's on the same set of target queries. The competitive citation gap is the primary strategic target for GEO — identifying which specific queries your competitors win and you don't, and closing that gap through content improvement, authority building, and structured data. A negative gap means you're losing AI mindshare to competitors.

Measurement

### AI Traffic

Website visits that originate from clicks within AI-generated answers. Currently a small but rapidly growing percentage of total traffic for most sites — but this metric understates the actual impact of AI visibility, since most AI answers are consumed without a click. AI traffic should be tracked separately in analytics (via referrer data from ChatGPT, Perplexity, etc.) and reported alongside share of voice metrics.

Measurement

### Non-Determinism (AI)

The property that identical prompts submitted to an AI system can produce different outputs — including different source citations — each time. Non-determinism means that AI citation tracking requires statistical sampling across many runs, not a single test. A citation rate of 70% means your content was cited 7 out of 10 times on that query — not that it always or never appears.

Measurement

### Zero-Click Rate

The proportion of searches that end without the user clicking through to any external website, because their query was answered directly on the SERP or in the AI response. Zero-click rates on AI-heavy queries dramatically exceed those on traditional search. This means traditional traffic metrics will undercount AI-influenced brand awareness — marketers need to supplement click-based analytics with survey-based brand attribution studies.

Measurement

### AI Brand Sentiment

The tone and framing with which AI systems describe your brand when mentioning it in responses. AI systems don't just cite brands — they contextualise them. Appearing as "a leading provider of X" carries very different brand value than "X company, which has faced criticism for Y." Monitoring AI brand sentiment across platforms is an emerging reputation management discipline closely related to GEO.

## Section 6: 9 Query, Intent & Retrieval Terms

How users talk to AI — and how AI retrieves answers. Understanding the query layer is essential for content targeting.

Query

### Conversational Query

A search request phrased in natural, full-sentence language rather than keyword fragments. "What are the best B2B SaaS marketing strategies for a Series A startup?" rather than "B2B SaaS marketing." AI-era queries average 20+ words. Content optimized for conversational queries uses natural language, addresses context, and anticipates follow-up questions.

Query

### Multi-Turn Conversation

An AI search interaction involving multiple sequential follow-up questions, allowing users to refine and deepen a line of inquiry within a single session. Multi-turn conversations mean that being cited in the first response dramatically increases the likelihood of being cited in subsequent responses in the same session. Comprehensive content that anticipates follow-up questions captures this reinforcement effect.

**PRO TIP:** Build "conversation-ready" content that answers the likely next 2–3 questions after your primary answer.

Query

### Fan-Out Queries

When an AI system breaks a single user prompt into multiple related sub-queries, runs them in parallel, and synthesizes the combined results. Google Deep Search (within AI Mode) can generate hundreds of sub-queries for a single complex question. Fan-out architecture means that topically comprehensive content — covering many related sub-questions on one page — has a higher probability of being retrieved for at least one of the sub-queries.

Query

### Informational Query

A query seeking factual knowledge or explanation: "What is GEO?", "How does Perplexity work?", "What are the benefits of structured data?" Informational queries are the dominant use case for AI search — they represent the highest volume opportunity for GEO citation. Definitions, explanations, comparisons, and how-to content are the content formats that win informational queries.

Query

### Transactional Query (AI)

A query with commercial purchase intent — "best GEO agency in London," "GEO services pricing," "compare GEO platforms." Transactional queries are increasingly being handled by AI systems in 2026, with product recommendations and service comparisons appearing in AI Overviews and ChatGPT responses. Optimizing category and product pages for AI citation on transactional queries is a direct revenue-generation GEO tactic.

Query

### Search Grounding

Forcing an LLM to retrieve live web evidence before generating its answer, rather than relying solely on training data. Search grounding reduces AI hallucination and ties responses to verifiable, citable sources. From a GEO perspective, well-grounded AI systems are more likely to cite accurately — but also more competitive, as only genuinely authoritative content survives the grounding retrieval step.

Query

### Semantic Search

Search that matches conceptual meaning and user intent rather than exact keyword strings. As search and AI converge, semantic understanding has become the baseline expectation. A page about "GEO" can now rank for "how to appear in AI answers," "AI search optimisation," and "generative engine visibility" — without containing those exact phrases — if its semantic content is strong and contextually rich.

Query

### Featured Snippet

The extracted answer Google highlights at the top of traditional search results, above all organic rankings. Often the first piece of content AI Overviews reference when forming their answers for navigational and informational queries where Google has strong traditional ranking signals. Winning featured snippets on your target queries remains one of the strongest predictors of AI Overviews citation.

Query

### SERP (AI Era Definition)

Search Engine Results Page. In 2026, a Google SERP for many queries blends: AI Overviews (top), Featured Snippets, People Also Ask boxes, traditional organic results, and rich results (maps, reviews, products) — sometimes with AI Mode accessible as a separate tab. Understanding the modern SERP composition helps prioritize which GEO tactics drive the most visible results for your target queries.

## Section 7: 7 UX & Quality Signal Terms

The page-level and site-level signals that tell AI systems your content is authoritative, trustworthy, and worth citing.

Quality

### Answer Fidelity

How accurately a piece of content answers a specific question — without off-topic padding, misleading framing, or critical omissions. High answer fidelity is the core content quality signal for GEO. AI systems select sources that best match the user's actual informational need, not sources that merely contain related keywords. Write to answer the question fully and precisely, then stop.

Quality

### Corroboration

Agreement between your content's claims and what other authoritative sources state. Generative AI systems cross-reference multiple sources for consistency before citing any individual one — claims that align with established consensus are significantly more likely to be included in synthesized answers. Disagreeing with expert consensus without strong evidence reduces citation probability regardless of content quality.

Quality

### First-Hand Experience Signal

Content demonstrating that the author personally experienced, tested, used, or investigated the subject being described. The "E" (Experience) in E-E-A-T. AI engines increasingly distinguish between first-hand and second-hand knowledge — content written by practitioners with direct experience consistently outperforms content aggregated from other sources. Case studies, original data, and practitioner observations are high-value experience signals.

**PRO TIP:** Include author bylines with credentials, client results, and original data wherever possible. AI systems attribute credibility at the entity level.

Quality

### Specificity

The level of concrete, quantified detail in a piece of content. GEO rewards specificity: "37% of enterprise marketers increased AI search budget in Q1 2026" is more citable than "many marketers are investing in AI search." Specific data is verifiable, quotable, and trusted by AI synthesis systems — vague generalisations are filtered out in competitive query environments.

Quality

### Topical Completeness

How comprehensively a piece of content covers all relevant dimensions of a topic. AI engines favour sources that provide complete answers over sources addressing only one facet of a multi-part question. For complex queries ("how to build a GEO strategy for an e-commerce brand"), a comprehensive page that addresses research, content architecture, technical setup, and measurement will consistently outperform narrow pages covering only one element.

Quality

### Core Web Vitals

Google's three user experience metrics: Largest Contentful Paint (LCP — loading speed), Interaction to Next Paint (INP — interactivity), and Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS — visual stability). Core Web Vitals are direct Google ranking signals and indirectly affect GEO — pages with poor CWVs are crawled less frequently, may rank lower in traditional search (reducing AI Overviews citation probability), and create poor experiences that undermine E-E-A-T signals.

Quality

### Earned Media

Third-party coverage, press mentions, citations, and references to your brand across independent publications — not owned or paid. Research from GEO studies (including the University of Toronto 2025 paper) demonstrates that AI search systems show a systematic bias towards earned media over brand-owned content. Press mentions, academic citations, industry directory listings, and journalist quotes all function as "AI authority signals" in ways that traditional SEO has not previously prioritized.

**PRO TIP:** Your PR strategy is now your GEO strategy. Third-party coverage is the highest-value input for LLM citation authority.

## Section 8: 5 Strategic GEO Terms

The higher-level strategic concepts that frame how GEO programmes are built, managed, and scaled.

Strategy

### GEO Audit

A structured assessment of a brand's current visibility across AI search platforms — measuring citation rates, share of voice, content gaps, technical barriers, and competitive positioning. A GEO audit benchmarks where you stand today and identifies the highest-priority improvements for maximum citation uplift. GO MO Group's GEO audit framework is purpose-built for B2B organizations, covering all major AI platforms and delivering a prioritized action roadmap tailored to your industry and buyer journey.

**PRO TIP:** A GEO audit is the essential first step. You cannot optimize what you have not measured.

Strategy

### Content Gap Analysis (AI)

The process of identifying which queries in your target topic area your content does not currently address — creating gaps that competitors fill in AI-generated responses. AI content gap analysis differs from traditional keyword gap analysis: you are identifying questions AI systems are asked that your brand has no citable answer to, rather than keywords your pages don't rank for. Prioritise gaps in high-commercial-intent query clusters first.

Strategy

### Entity Optimization

The practice of ensuring that your brand, products, key personnel, and topics are accurately and consistently represented as named entities across the web — in Wikipedia, Wikidata, Google Business Profile, industry directories, and authoritative external references. Entity optimization shapes how AI systems "understand" your brand independently of any specific page, making it a foundational LLMO strategy that supports all GEO and AEO efforts.

Strategy

### Big Brand Bias

The documented tendency of AI search systems to disproportionately cite large, established brands over smaller or newer entrants — even when smaller brand content is more accurate, recent, or directly relevant. Identified in GEO research as a systemic challenge for challenger brands. Counterstrategies include targeting specific niche query clusters where brand authority is less of a differentiator, and aggressively building earned media signals that signal authority within a specific domain.

Strategy

### AI-First Content Strategy

A content planning and production approach where AI citation potential — not keyword rankings — is the primary optimisation objective. AI-first content is structured around question clusters, features direct answers at the head of each section, incorporates original data as citation anchors, uses schema markup systematically, and measures success through share of voice metrics rather than rank position. Represents the evolution of "content marketing" for the AI search era.

## Section 9: The 4 Major AI Crawlers & What They Index

These are the bots crawling your site for AI training and real-time retrieval. Know each one — and check your robots.txt isn't blocking them.

**GPTBot** OpenAI

Crawls web content for ChatGPT training data and ChatGPT Search retrieval. Block via robots.txt with "User-agent: GPTBot" if you don't want OpenAI to use your content. Allowing access increases ChatGPT citation potential.

**ClaudeBot**

Anthropic

Anthropic's crawler for Claude training and retrieval. Crawls content to build Claude's understanding of web sources. Selectively indexes based on content quality signals. Controllable via robots.txt.

**PerplexityBot**

Perplexity AI

Powers Perplexity's real-time citation engine. Because Perplexity's citation behavior is transparent and trackable, PerplexityBot crawl access is particularly valuable for GEO visibility measurement and citation building.

**Google-Extended**

Google

Google's dedicated crawler for Gemini training and AI Overviews retrieval, separate from Googlebot. Can be blocked independently via robots.txt if you want Google's traditional search to index you but not use your content for AI training.

> **Critical Robots.txt Audit Required**
>
> Check your robots.txt immediately. Many sites have blanket bot-blocking rules — often inherited from generic templates or over-cautious legacy settings — that are blocking all AI crawlers and silently eliminating GEO visibility. If GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, and Google-Extended are blocked, you are invisible to AI search. This is the single most common "quick win" in a GO MO GEO audit.
