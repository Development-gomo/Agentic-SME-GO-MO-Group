# Google Has Published Its Official AI Search Optimisation Guide. Here’s What It Actually Says.

For the first time, Google has released a dedicated resource on optimising content for its generative AI features, including AI Overviews and AI Mode. We've read every line, so you can spend your time acting on it. Below you'll find the plain-English breakdown, the myths Google has put to rest, and what it all means for your strategy.

![](https://www.gomogroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/google-ai-search-optimization.webp)

## **The short version**

Google has published its first official guide to appearing in generative AI features on Search, and the news is encouraging. **Good SEO is still the foundation.** Create genuinely useful, original content, keep your technical house in order, and feel free to ignore most of the "AI SEO hacks" doing the rounds online. In Google's own words, GEO is still SEO. If you've been investing in quality, you're already on the right path.

## **Why this guide matters**

Google's generative AI features, AI Overviews and AI Mode, have changed how millions of people search. Until now, though, official guidance for site owners who want to appear in them has been thin on the ground.

In May 2026, Google Search Central published [a dedicated optimisation guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) alongside the SEO Starter Guide in its core documentation. That placement says a lot. Optimising for generative AI search is now a first-class priority for web publishers, not a niche technical side project.

The guide covers three areas: which foundational SEO practices carry over, what you don't need to do, and how to think about the emerging "agentic" AI experiences. Here's our breakdown.

RAG

The technique Google uses to ground AI answers. It pulls from the core Search index, not from a separate AI database.

AIO

AI Overviews. The AI-generated summaries shown above traditional results for many queries.

GEO

Generative Engine Optimisation. What Google calls “still SEO”, with a renewed focus on content quality and structure.

## **SEO is still the foundation, and Google says so clearly**

The guide opens with a direct answer: yes, SEO still matters for generative AI search. There's no hedging. Google explains that AI Overviews and AI Mode are built on the same ranking and quality systems that power traditional Search.

The mechanism behind this is called **retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)**. Instead of writing answers from scratch, the AI retrieves relevant, indexed web pages and builds its response from them. For your content to be considered, it needs to be in the index and meet Google's quality signals. The work you've already put into your site counts.

> _"From Google Search's perspective, optimising for generative AI search is optimising for the search experience, and thus still SEO."_
>
> **Google Search Central, May 2026**

Google also describes a second mechanism, **query fan-out**. Here the AI model generates several related sub-queries to build a complete answer. A user asking "how to fix a lawn full of weeds" might trigger sub-queries about herbicides, chemical-free removal and weed prevention. Pages that cover specific, well-defined topics clearly have a better chance of being picked up by those sub-queries.

## **What you should actually do**

### **1\. Create genuinely original content**

This is Google's main message, and it plays to the strengths of anyone with real expertise. The guide separates **commodity content** (common knowledge that's easy to produce with AI and adds no unique insight) from **non-commodity content** (expert or experienced perspectives that go beyond what's available elsewhere).

Google's example makes the point well. "7 Tips for First-Time Homebuyers" is commodity. "Why We Waived the Inspection and Saved Money: A Look Inside the Sewer Line" is not. The second piece comes from lived experience, offers a distinct point of view and is hard to copy. That's exactly the kind of content AI systems are built to surface, and exactly the kind your organisation is able to create.

01

Provide a unique point of viewFirst-hand reviews, original research and expert analysis. Content that only you can produce.

02

Write for human readers firstParagraphs, sections and clear headings. The structure people enjoy reading is the same structure AI can parse.

03

Support text with high-quality images and videoAI Overviews can include visual content. If you already follow image and video SEO best practice, you're optimising for this too.

04

Focus on your visitors' satisfactionGoogle's systems are designed to connect people with content that satisfies them. If you'd be proud to show a visitor your page, you're on the right track.

### **2\. Keep your technical foundation solid**

To appear in generative AI features, a page must be indexed and eligible to appear in Google Search with a snippet. That's the starting point. From there, Google repeats its standard technical guidance: crawlability, clean JavaScript handling, good page experience and less duplicate content.

One detail worth knowing: Google says you don't need perfectly semantic HTML, but semantic markup is still a smart choice. It helps screen readers and accessibility tools, and it makes your pages easier for the new generation of AI agents to navigate (more on that below).

### **3\. Use Merchant Center and Google Business Profile where relevant**

For e-commerce and local businesses, Google's generative AI answers can surface product listings and business information. Merchant Center feeds and Google Business Profiles put that data directly in front of the systems generating those answers. If you haven't set these up yet, it's one of the quickest wins available.

## **What you can stop worrying about**

This may be the most useful part of the guide. Google directly addresses the most common "GEO hacks" being promoted across the industry and confirms that you can leave them off your list:

✕

LLMS.txt files and special AI markupYou don't need to create llms.txt or any other machine-readable AI files. Google may index many file types, but none of them get special treatment in AI features.

✕

"Chunking" content into small piecesGoogle's systems understand multiple topics on a single page. There's no ideal page length, and splitting content into fragments for AI isn't required or particularly helpful.

✕

Rewriting content specifically for AI systemsAI understands synonyms and overall meaning. You don't need to add long-tail keyword variations or rewrite pages to match every possible phrasing of a query.

✕

Seeking inauthentic "mentions" across the webManufactured coverage in forums, blogs or third-party sites doesn't reliably influence AI answers, and it risks breaching spam policies.

✕

Over-investing in structured data for AIStructured data isn't required for generative AI features, and there's no special schema to add. It's still valuable for rich results, but it isn't a lever for AI visibility.

### **GO MO perspective: The fundamentals are your competitive advantage.**

Much of what's sold online as "AEO" or "GEO strategy" relies on tactics Google has now explicitly ruled out. That's good news for anyone who prefers doing things properly. The opportunity isn't in finding new tricks. It's in doing the fundamentals better than competitors who are busy chasing shortcuts. Original expertise, clean architecture and genuine helpfulness: that's the playbook, and it's one you can own.

## **The agentic frontier**

The most forward-looking section of the guide covers AI agents. These are autonomous systems that browse the web on a user's behalf to complete tasks such as booking a table, comparing products or gathering research. Google describes browser agents that analyse screenshots, inspect DOM structure and navigate using accessibility trees.

Google's advice here is measured. This is still emerging, and you only need to engage with it "if it's relevant to your business and you have extra time." The direction is clear, though. Websites that are well-structured, accessible and semantically clean will be easier for these agents to work with, which gives early movers a real head start.

New protocols such as the **Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP)** are emerging to standardise how agents transact with websites. This space will grow in importance, especially for e-commerce and service businesses.

## **What this means for B2B marketers**

Google's guide is written for a broad audience, but the implications for B2B deserve their own spotlight.

**Content depth matters more than content volume.** B2B buyers ask complex, specific questions that commodity content can't answer. One well-researched article built on internal data, client experience or genuine technical knowledge is worth more, for AI citations and for conversions, than a library of generic posts. Your expertise is the asset.

**Technical buyers are increasingly AI-mediated.** Someone researching industrial equipment, SaaS platforms or professional services is now likely to see an AI Overview before a traditional result. Earning a citation there takes exactly what the guide describes: authoritative, original, well-structured content.

**The "AI SEO agency" market is full of noise.** Now that Google has said clearly that llms.txt files, content chunking and manufactured mentions don't work, B2B marketing leaders can confidently question any vendor leading with them as differentiators.

### **The practical checklist**

Is your content indexed and snippet-eligible? Is it genuinely original, built on expertise or experience your competitors don't have? Is the page technically clean, crawlable and accessible? Are you using Google Business Profile or Merchant Center where relevant? **If you can answer yes to all four, you're well positioned for generative AI search.** Everything else is secondary.
